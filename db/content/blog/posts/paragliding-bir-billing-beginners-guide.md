Standing on the edge of the Billing take-off at 2,400 metres, with the Dhauladhar range spreading out ahead and the Tibetan colony of Bir a kilometre below, is one of those experiences that rewires your sense of what is possible. This guide covers everything a first-timer needs — when to go, what a tandem flight actually involves, how much to budget, and how to pick an operator you can trust — so you step off that ridge feeling prepared, not anxious.

## Why Bir Billing Has Earned Its Reputation

[Bir Billing](/destinations/bir-billing) sits in the Kangra district of Himachal Pradesh, tucked between the Dhauladhar and the outer Himalayan ranges. The geography is almost absurdly well-suited to paragliding: the take-off at Billing (roughly 2,400 m) feeds into a long, clean glide path down to the landing zone at Bir (around 1,400 m), giving pilots a consistent 1,000-metre vertical descent over terrain that generates reliable thermal columns for most of the year.

The site's credentials are not just word-of-mouth. It hosted the Paragliding World Cup in 2015, drawing elite pilots from across the world, and it is consistently ranked among the top paragliding destinations globally. What makes it exceptional is the combination of altitude, thermal quality, and the length of cross-country routes available to licensed pilots — some fly well over 100 km on a single flight here. For first-timers on a tandem, that same geography means a flight that feels substantial and memorable rather than a brief loop over a hillside.

## Best Time to Go — and When to Avoid

The flying calendar at Bir Billing has two clear windows.

**October to November** is widely considered the prime season. Post-monsoon air is dry and stable, thermals are predictable, and the light on the mountains in autumn is genuinely spectacular. Visibility is usually excellent, and pilots report some of the smoothest conditions of the year. If you have flexibility in your schedule, aim for this window.

**March to June** is the other reliable season. Spring thermals are strong — sometimes very strong — which experienced pilots love for cross-country distance. For beginners on a tandem, conditions can be lively in the afternoons, so morning slots (before 11 am) tend to be smoother. The landscape in spring, with rhododendrons in bloom, is beautiful.

**July to September (monsoon) is closed** for commercial flying at most reputable operators. Cloud cover, unpredictable wind, and wet conditions make it unsafe. Some operators may still pitch flights during a brief dry patch in the monsoon — treat that as a red flag, not an opportunity.

If you arrive and conditions are not right, expect your slot to be delayed or rescheduled. Weather dependency is simply part of paragliding; any operator who guarantees a flight regardless of conditions is one to avoid.

## What Actually Happens on a Tandem Flight

A [tandem paragliding](/activities/paragliding) flight requires no prior experience from you. Here is the sequence:

1. You arrive at Billing (usually by jeep from Bir — a rough 45-minute climb).
2. The pilot gives a briefing covering the harness, take-off run, and landing procedure.
3. You are clipped into the harness in front of the pilot, who controls the glider throughout.
4. Take-off involves a short run into the wind — typically five to ten steps — and then you are airborne.
5. The flight lasts roughly **10 to 30 minutes** depending on the package you have booked and the thermals available. Some operators offer extended flights.
6. Landing is at the designated zone in Bir village, generally a smooth affair on flat ground.

That is genuinely it on your side. Your job is to run when the pilot says run, sit back in the harness once airborne, and look around. The pilot manages launch, flight path, thermals, and landing entirely.

## Physical Requirements and Who Should Not Fly

Tandem flights at Bir Billing are open to a wide age range, but operators apply weight and health limits that exist for legitimate safety reasons. Typical restrictions:

- **Weight limit**: most operators cap passengers at around 90–100 kg; a few go to 110 kg on specific equipment. Confirm this before booking.
- **Minimum age**: usually around 5–7 years, with parental consent required for minors.
- **Health conditions**: active heart conditions, epilepsy, severe back or neck problems, and pregnancy are standard contraindications. If in doubt, speak to your doctor and disclose your situation to the operator.
- **Fear of heights**: counterintuitively, many people who are afraid of heights find tandem paragliding manageable because the sensation in an open harness is not the same as standing on a ledge. That said, only you know yourself.

If an operator waves away your health questions or does not ask about weight, that is a warning sign.

## How Much Does It Cost

Expect to pay approximately **₹2,000 to ₹3,500** for a standard tandem joy ride. The range reflects variation in flight duration, season, and whether a GoPro video package is included. Video footage adds roughly ₹300–₹800 to most packages.

Extended flights or premium slots with well-regarded pilots may cost more. These figures are approximate — prices shift with season and operator. Always confirm the exact current price directly with your operator at the time of booking.

A few things worth budgeting for beyond the flight itself:

- Jeep transfer up to Billing: usually ₹150–₹300 per person
- Accommodation in Bir: ranges from ₹600 guesthouses to ₹3,000+ boutique properties
- A day's margin in your itinerary for a weather delay

## Choosing an Operator You Can Actually Trust

This is the most important section of this guide. Paragliding is an adventure sport with genuine risk, and the difference between a safe flight and a dangerous one almost always comes down to equipment condition and pilot certification.

**Check for certification.** Pilots should hold a valid certification from the Aero Club of India or an internationally recognised body (such as CIVL or BHPA). Do not be embarrassed to ask for it.

**Inspect the equipment.** A reputable operator will show you the harness, glider, and reserve parachute without hesitation and will have documented maintenance records. A glider in use at Bir Billing should be inspected and certified regularly.

**Attend the full briefing.** Any operator who rushes the pre-flight briefing to get more passengers through is cutting corners. The briefing is short — ten to fifteen minutes — but it matters.

**Avoid touts at the landing zone.** Bir has a well-known issue with unlicensed operators soliciting passengers informally. Book through a verified platform or directly with a named, licensed operator whose credentials you have checked.

That does not replace your own due diligence, but it removes a meaningful layer of uncertainty before you even arrive.

## Getting to Bir Billing

The nearest commercial airport is Gaggal (Kangra Airport), roughly 60–70 km from Bir. Several airlines operate Delhi–Gaggal flights; from the airport, taxis to Bir take about 90 minutes.

Alternatively, Pathankot (in Punjab) is the nearest major rail junction, about 120–130 km away. Buses and taxis from Pathankot are the most common route for budget travellers. The Kangra Valley Railway from Pathankot to Jogindernagar is a narrow-gauge line that passes through beautiful terrain — slow, but worth it if you have the time.

From Bir village, jeep operators shuttle passengers up to the Billing take-off point every morning during the flying season. Confirm timings with your paragliding operator the night before, since morning slots fill fast.

## What to Wear and Pack

Dress in layers. Even in April, Billing at 2,400 m is significantly colder than the valley below. A fleece or light down jacket over a base layer is the minimum; in October–November, add a windproof outer layer.

- **Footwear**: lace-up shoes or light hiking boots with ankle support; avoid sandals or slip-ons.
- **Sunglasses**: essential — UV is intense at altitude.
- **A small daypack** is fine; your operator will store it during the flight.
- Leave loose items (scarves, caps, open bags) secured or behind — they become hazards in the wind.
- If you wear glasses, ask your pilot about a strap or goggles.

Your operator will provide the harness and helmet. You do not need to bring your own equipment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is paragliding in Bir Billing safe for first-timers with no experience?

Tandem flights are specifically designed for passengers with no experience — the certified pilot manages the entire flight. Safety is not guaranteed in any adventure sport, but the risk is kept manageable when you fly with a certified pilot, on well-maintained equipment, in suitable weather conditions, and after attending the full pre-flight briefing. The key variables in your control are operator choice and honest disclosure of any health conditions.

### Can I fly alone, or does it have to be tandem?

If you are a licensed paraglider with a recognised certification, solo flights are possible and Bir Billing is one of the finest cross-country sites in the world. For anyone without a licence, all commercial flights are tandem with a certified pilot. There are paragliding schools in Bir if you want to learn — basic courses typically run 5–7 days and cover ground handling before any air time.

### What if the weather is bad when I arrive?

Flights are weather-dependent and can be delayed or cancelled on the day. Reputable operators will not fly in unsafe wind conditions regardless of how far you have travelled. Build at least one extra day into your itinerary and check whether your booking comes with a free cancellation or rescheduling option if conditions are unfit.

### How early should I book, especially in October–November?

Peak season (October–November) fills up quickly, particularly for morning slots when conditions are calmest. Booking two to four weeks in advance is advisable for the best operator availability. If you are travelling as a group, book even earlier.
