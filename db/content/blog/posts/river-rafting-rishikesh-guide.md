The Ganga tears out of the Himalayas at Rishikesh with enough force to humble the most seasoned paddler, yet gently enough in places to welcome a first-timer gripping a paddle for the very first time. This guide walks you through every decision you will face — which stretch to book, when to go, what to expect on the water, how to stay safe, and roughly what you will pay — so you can step into that raft with eyes wide open.

## Why Rishikesh Is India's White-Water Capital

[Rishikesh](/destinations/rishikesh) sits at the point where the Ganga emerges from the Shivalik foothills onto the Gangetic plain in Uttarakhand. The geography does something special here: a series of gorges, boulders, and gradient drops spaced across roughly 36 kilometres of river create a natural progression of rapids ranging from calm and glittery to genuinely technical. You get glacial green water, forested canyon walls, and a basecamp town with easy road access from Delhi (about 240 km) and Dehradun (about 45 km). No other stretch of river in India packs this variety, this accessibility, and this ecosystem of experienced operators into one place. That combination is why [river rafting in Rishikesh](/adventure/rafting-in-rishikesh) has become a benchmark experience for Indian adventure travellers.

## Understanding Rapid Grades Before You Choose a Stretch

Rapid grades are a universal shorthand for difficulty, and knowing them will save you from booking the wrong stretch.

- **Grade I** — Fast-moving flat water, small ripples, no real obstacles. Anyone can paddle through.
- **Grade II** — Easy rapids with clear channels and low waves. Good for families and first-timers.
- **Grade III** — Moderate rapids with irregular waves, some manoeuvring required. Suitable for most healthy adults.
- **Grade IV** — Powerful, predictable rapids requiring precise boat control. Prior experience or a strong guide is essential.
- **Grade V** — Violent, complex rapids with serious hazard. Expert paddlers and guides only.

The Rishikesh stretches span Grades I through IV, which means there is a genuinely appropriate option for every skill level — but matching yourself to the right grade matters both for safety and enjoyment.

## The Four Main Stretches and Their Famous Rapids

### Brahmpuri — 9 km, Grade I–II

Starting from Brahmpuri and ending at Shivpuri, this is the gentlest run on the circuit. The water is forgiving, the rapids are small, and the guide has time to teach you proper paddle strokes without anyone panicking. If you are travelling with children (most operators enforce a 14-year minimum age), older family members, or people who are simply uncertain about their comfort in moving water, start here. Price is typically the lowest of the four stretches, roughly **₹600–₹900 per person** — confirm the current rate with your operator at booking.

### Shivpuri — 16 km, Grade III–IV

This is the classic Rishikesh run and the one most people mean when they say they went rafting in Rishikesh. It starts at Marine Drive or Shivpuri (operators vary) and ends at Nim Beach or Ram Jhula. The big names all live on this stretch: **Three Blind Mice** arrives early and is a playful warm-up; **Roller Coaster** is the crowd favourite — a long, bouncy wave train that usually leaves everyone grinning or briefly missing their paddle; **Golf Course** offers a technical read with a clean line through a boulder garden; and **The Wall** is the rapid that earns its name with a pushy hydraulic that can flip an unprepared raft. Expect to be wet, to laugh loudly, and to feel genuinely accomplished at the take-out. Budget roughly **₹1,000–₹1,600 per person**.

### Marine Drive — 24–26 km, Grade III–IV

A longer day on the water, beginning higher up and rolling through more mileage before reaching the lower rapids. You get everything on the Shivpuri stretch plus additional gradient and time in the gorge. **Body Surfing** is the highlight that most guides offer here — you slip out of the raft in a calm pool wearing your life jacket and float a short rapid section on your back, feet downstream, which is equal parts terrifying and exhilarating. Only attempt this when your guide judges conditions safe. Approximate price: **₹1,500–₹2,000 per person**.

### Kaudiyala — ~36 km, Grade III–IV

The full run from Kaudiyala to Rishikesh is the longest single-day rafting stretch on the circuit and an all-day commitment. You cover the most remote sections of the gorge, pass through quieter canyon corridors, and eventually arrive at the famous lower rapids with tired arms and very wide smiles. This stretch suits people who want the fullest possible day on the river and are comfortable with extended time in a raft. Prices for the full run typically fall in the **₹2,000–₹2,500 per person** range, sometimes including a riverside lunch stop — verify inclusions before you book.

## When to Go: The Rafting Season and What Closes It

The season runs roughly from **late September through mid-to-late June**. Within that window, the experience shifts meaningfully by month.

- **October to November** — Post-monsoon flows are healthy, the gorge is green, air temperatures are comfortable, and crowds are moderate. One of the best windows.
- **December to February** — The water is cold (genuinely cold — wetsuits are advisable and most operators provide them or rent them). Flows are lower and cleaner. Fewer tourists means better logistics on the river.
- **March to May** — Rising temperatures make the experience more pleasant post-dunking. Snowmelt from higher elevations starts to swell the river through April and May, increasing power and pace. Grades effectively feel stiffer during peak flows.
- **June** — The tail end of the season. Flows are high and the rapids punch harder. Operators often cancel or restrict access when levels cross safety thresholds — always check conditions with your operator a day before travel.
- **July to August** — The river closes. Monsoon rains turn the Ganga into an unpredictable, debris-laden torrent. No legitimate operator runs trips in this period, and you should not attempt the river regardless of what anyone tells you.

## Safety: What Certified Operators Do Differently

Safety on the Ganga is not optional theatre — it is the difference between a great story and a medical emergency. Here is what you should expect from any operator worth booking with:

1. A **certified guide** leading your raft — look for IFR (International Federation of Rafting) or equivalent certification, or guides trained through state-recognised programs.
2. A **personal flotation device (PFD/life jacket)** that fits correctly and is fastened before you enter the water — not draped over your shoulders.
3. A **helmet** for every person on the raft on Grade III and above sections.
4. A **safety kayak** or safety raft accompanying the group, positioned downstream of difficult rapids to assist swimmers.
5. A **safety briefing** before the put-in. Your guide will explain swimming position (feet downstream, on your back), self-rescue, paddle commands, and what to do if the raft flips. Pay full attention. This is not a legal formality — it is information you may actually need.

A few non-negotiables from your side: do not raft if you have a heart condition, epilepsy, or a recent serious injury without explicit medical clearance. Most operators enforce a **minimum age of 14 years** and weight/health restrictions — ask in advance. Do not drink alcohol before or during the trip. Respect the guide's call to skip or modify a section; that call is based on real-time water levels and your group's ability, not conservatism.

## What to Wear and Bring

Wear clothes you do not mind getting completely wet: a quick-dry synthetic top and shorts or tights work well. Avoid jeans (they become heavy and cold). Secure your footwear — closed-toe sandals or old trainers that can get wet are best; flip-flops will be lost in the first rapid. Tie back long hair. Leave valuables, your good phone, and your wallet in a secure dry bag or at your accommodation. Most operators provide waterproof dry bags for essentials. Sunscreen applied before the briefing is sensible on sunny days.

## Booking: What to Look For and Rough Pricing

Prices across the four stretches currently run approximately **₹600 at the lower end to ₹2,500 at the upper end** per person, varying by stretch length, group size, inclusions (meals, transport, wetsuits), and operator. These are approximate ranges — always confirm the live price directly with the operator at the time of booking, as rates shift with season and demand.

When choosing an operator, verify that guides hold recognised certification, that life jackets and helmets are in sound condition (not cracked, faded to brittleness, or missing buckles), and that a safety kayak is part of the setup. Look for operators with transparent cancellation policies. [Rafting trips](/activities/rafting) booked through Switchback list KYC-verified operators, so you can see credentials before you commit.

Avoid any operator who offers to skip the safety briefing, who seems casual about life jackets, or who applies heavy pressure to upsell you to a harder stretch than you are comfortable with. The river will still be there tomorrow.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to know how to swim to go rafting in Rishikesh?

You do not need to be a strong swimmer, but basic water comfort helps. Your life jacket will keep you afloat if you fall out, and the guide and safety kayaker are trained to retrieve swimmers quickly. That said, if you are completely non-swimmer and anxious in water, the Brahmpuri stretch (Grade I–II) is the most appropriate starting point, and you should tell your guide before the trip begins.

### Can children raft in Rishikesh?

Most operators set a minimum age of 14 years, and some raise it to 16 for the more powerful Grade III–IV stretches. There is no universal fixed rule — policies vary by operator and by stretch. If you are travelling with younger teenagers, call the operator directly to confirm eligibility before you travel.

### Is the water clean enough to be in?

The Ganga at Rishikesh, above the main town discharge points, is considerably cleaner than it is further downstream. You will swallow some water — everyone does. It is not a medical crisis for most healthy adults. If you have a compromised immune system or open wounds, take sensible precautions and consult a doctor if you develop any symptoms after the trip.

### What happens if the trip gets cancelled due to high water?

Responsible operators monitor river gauges and will cancel or reschedule when levels exceed safe thresholds, particularly in June and post-rain periods in October. Confirm the cancellation and refund policy at the time of booking. Switchback operators are required to maintain transparent cancellation terms, so you will know exactly where you stand before you pay.
