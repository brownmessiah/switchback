Most people who've done a tandem paragliding flight will tell you the same thing: the part they were most afraid of turned out to be nothing, and the part that actually surprised them was how *quiet* it was up there. This is a step-by-step walkthrough of exactly what happens on your first flight — from the moment you arrive at the take-off hill to the moment your feet hit the landing field — so you can show up calm, prepared, and ready to enjoy it.

## What Tandem Paragliding Actually Is (and Isn't)

In a tandem flight, you are harnessed in front of a certified pilot who sits behind you and controls the glider entirely. You are a passenger. Your job is to run a few steps on take-off, sit back into the harness, and look at the view. You do not need to know anything about flying, and you do not need any prior experience. There is no minimum fitness level for most healthy adults, though operators enforce weight limits — typically up to around 90–100 kg depending on the pilot, glider rating, and site — so check the specific limit when you book [paragliding flights](/activities/paragliding).

## The Take-Off: You Run, You Don't Jump

This is the part beginners most often misread in videos. You do not leap off a cliff. At a site like Billing, the launch hill slopes away at an angle, and take-off is essentially a short, committed downhill run — usually five to ten steps — while the glider inflates and lifts above you. The wing does the work. By the time you feel the harness pulling you upward, you are already airborne without having noticed the exact moment it happened.

At Billing, the launch sits at roughly 2,400 metres above sea level. You will feel the altitude before the flight — the air is thinner and colder than you expect, even in May. Your pilot will talk you through every step: where to stand, how to hold the risers loosely, when to start running, and critically, to keep running until the glider fully lifts you. The most common first-timer mistake is stopping mid-run out of hesitation. Trust the instructions, commit to the run, and the rest happens on its own.

## Gear Check and the Safety Briefing — Pay Attention Here

Your pilot will fit a harness over your clothing, check every buckle, and clip you directly in front of them with carabiner connections. This takes a few minutes and the pilot will tug everything firm. Ask questions if anything feels wrong or unclear — a good pilot welcomes this.

The pre-flight briefing covers: the run-out cue, what to do with your legs at take-off (bent, then let them dangle), how to sit back once airborne (lean into the seat, not forward), and the landing approach (legs forward and together, then a short run-out or a gentle seat-slide). It is short and practical. Listen to it even if you have watched a hundred YouTube videos, because every site and every pilot has slightly different cues.

## In the Air: What the Flight Actually Feels Like

Once the wing lifts you and the slope drops away, the sensation most people describe is not fear — it is silence. Paragliding is quieter than you imagine. There is no engine, and without a wind directly in your face the soundscape is just air movement and occasionally the pilot's voice.

Expect a glide of roughly 10 to 30 minutes depending on conditions and the site. The pilot reads thermals — rising columns of warm air — to gain or maintain altitude. You may feel gentle banking turns as they work a thermal. This is normal and not turbulence in the aircraft sense; the wing is stable and the motion is gradual. If you feel queasy, tell the pilot immediately and they will bring you down on a more direct line. If you want something more exciting, ask for a spiral or wingover; a good pilot will read your comfort and calibrate accordingly.

[Tandem paragliding in Bir Billing](/adventure/paragliding-in-bir-billing) is one of the best-known experiences of this kind in India, partly because the Beas Kund bowl creates reliable thermals and long glide paths that let beginners actually enjoy the view rather than rushing a short hop. Flights from Billing to the Bir landing field cover a meaningful distance and give you the full experience.

## What to Wear and Carry

- Closed, lace-up shoes with a firm ankle — sandals, flip-flops, and slip-ons are not permitted.
- Warm layers: a fleece or light down jacket even in summer; temperatures at altitude can drop 10–15°C below the valley floor, and wind chill makes it feel colder still.
- Sunglasses with a strap or a snug fit — they will otherwise blow off in the first minute.
- Avoid a heavy meal in the two to three hours before the flight if you are prone to motion sickness. A light snack is fine.
- Leave large bags in the vehicle or at the operator's base; you cannot carry a full backpack during the flight.
- Ask about a GoPro video at booking. Most pilots offer helmet-cam footage for an additional charge (roughly ₹300–700; confirm current pricing with the operator). It is worth it — you will not remember the view as clearly as you think.

## Costs, Weather, and Booking Honestly

Tandem paragliding flights in India typically run anywhere from approximately ₹1,500 to ₹4,000 for a standard 10–20 minute flight, varying by site, season, altitude, and whether video is included. These are rough market ranges — always confirm the live price directly with the operator at the time of booking, as fuel, permit, and seasonal costs shift.

Weather is the one variable nobody controls. Flights pause or cancel for high wind, rain, or poor visibility. If the pilot or operator says the weather is not suitable, that call is non-negotiable — no reputable operator will fly in unsafe conditions, and you should be grateful for the caution rather than frustrated by the wait. Most operators will reschedule or refund a weather cancellation; confirm the policy before you pay. Booking through a platform with transparent cancellation terms is genuinely useful here.

## Who Should Not Fly

Tandem paragliding is not suitable for everyone, and being honest about this is more useful than a blanket reassurance. Check with the operator if you have: a heart condition, serious blood pressure issues, recent joint surgery, epilepsy, or severe vertigo. Pregnancy is a contraindication at most sites. Most operators set a minimum age of around 5–7 years with guardian consent, and a maximum weight of 90–100 kg (site-dependent). If you have any doubt, share your health history with the operator before paying — a KYC-verified operator will give you a straight answer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to know how to fly or have any training?

No training whatsoever is required for a tandem flight. You are harnessed to a certified pilot who controls the glider entirely. Your only active role is the short run on take-off and following the landing cue. First-timers with no prior outdoor activity experience fly routinely.

### Will I feel sick in the air?

Most people do not experience motion sickness on a calm tandem glide. If the pilot works thermals aggressively or does aerobatic manoeuvres it is more likely, so tell your pilot upfront that you prefer a calm flight if you are at all sensitive. Avoiding a heavy meal beforehand and keeping your gaze on the horizon rather than straight down both help.

### What happens if the weather turns bad mid-flight?

An experienced pilot monitors conditions constantly and will cut the flight short and land safely if conditions deteriorate. Landing is always possible sooner than the planned duration. This is one of the clearest reasons to fly only with a certified, experienced pilot — they read conditions before and during the flight, not just at launch.

### Is there a weight limit, and what if I am close to it?

Most tandem gliders used at Indian sites have an upper passenger weight of roughly 90–100 kg, but this varies by operator and pilot. If you are near the limit, ask specifically — the operator needs to match you with a suitable glider and pilot combination for safe flying. Do not guess or understate your weight; this is a safety parameter, not a comfort one.
