Standing on the edge of an 83-metre platform above the Ganges, harness tight, wind in your ears — that moment just before you jump is one of the most honest experiences you can have. This guide covers everything you need to know before booking a bungee jump in India: where to go, what it costs, who it suits, and how to make sure you come back grinning rather than rattled.

## The Three Main Bungee Jumping Destinations in India

India has a handful of dedicated bungee sites, but three stand out for infrastructure, operator experience, and consistency.

**Rishikesh** is the undisputed home of bungee jumping in India. The site at Mohanchatti runs a fixed platform at roughly 83 metres — long considered the country's highest fixed-platform jump. The setting is dramatic: a forested gorge above the Ganges, with trained jump masters on-site for every jump. If you're doing your first jump in India, or you want the benchmark experience, [Rishikesh](/destinations/rishikesh) is where most serious jumpers head.

**Goa** offers a crane-based jump of around 55 metres near Mayem Lake, which suits people who want the thrill with a slightly lower height and a beach-holiday context. The setup is seasonal and the vibe is more relaxed, but the safety requirements are identical.

**Lonavala and the Della Adventure Park** outside Pune runs crane jumps that draw a large crowd from Mumbai and Pune. It's more of an adventure park than a single-sport destination, which can mean queues on weekends but also makes it easy to combine with other activities.

Each site operates differently in terms of equipment, procedures, and capacity. Always confirm the current status of a site before you travel — crane-based operations especially can shift seasonally.

## What the Jump Actually Involves

A standard jump at any reputable Indian site uses both a full-body harness and an ankle harness together. This dual-attachment system is standard practice globally and significantly reduces the risk of cord-ankle separation. The bungee cord itself is rated for multiple jumps, but responsible operators retire and replace cords on a documented schedule.

Here's the sequence you can expect:

1. Weigh-in and registration — your weight determines which cord is used
2. A safety briefing from a trained jump master (listen carefully; this is not boilerplate)
3. Harness fitting, double-checked by at least one other staff member
4. Walk to the platform or crane, with a jump master alongside you
5. The jump, the rebound, and a controlled lowering to the recovery net or ground
6. Harness removal and a lot of adrenaline to deal with

The whole process from briefing to landing is typically 20–40 minutes. The free-fall itself lasts about three to four seconds.

## Costs: What to Budget

At the time of writing, bungee jumps at established sites in India generally run in the range of **₹3,500 to ₹4,000 per person** for the jump itself. Many operators offer a video add-on — a mounted camera records your jump and fall — for an additional fee, typically ₹500–₹1,500 depending on the site and package.

Prices can vary by season, demand, and whether you book in advance or walk in. A few things to factor in:

- Walk-in slots at popular sites like Rishikesh can be limited, especially in peak season (October to February). Booking ahead usually guarantees your slot.
- Group discounts exist at some sites but are not universal.
- The video is genuinely worth it — most people have almost no memory of the jump itself, and the footage is the only proof it happened.

Always confirm the current price directly with the operator at the time of booking. The figures above are typical as of mid-2026 but should not be treated as fixed.

## Who Can Jump — and Who Shouldn't

Weight limits exist for a precise reason: the bungee cord is calibrated to a weight range to control rebound force and cord extension. Standard limits at Indian sites are approximately **40 kg minimum and 110 kg maximum**. The minimum age is typically around 12 years, though some operators set it higher.

These are hard limits, not guidelines. A reputable jump master will turn you away if you fall outside the range, regardless of how much you want to jump.

The following conditions are generally disqualifying:

- Serious cardiac or heart conditions
- Back, neck, or spinal injuries or conditions
- Epilepsy or conditions causing loss of consciousness
- Pregnancy
- Recent surgery (typically within 3–6 months, but check with your operator and your doctor)
- High blood pressure that is not well-controlled

If you have any of these conditions, or any condition that affects your musculoskeletal or cardiovascular system, speak to your doctor before booking — not after. Some conditions are absolute contraindications; others may be fine with medical clearance. Do not guess.

For everyone else, bungee jumping is safe when run by a properly equipped, trained operator. The risks come primarily from unverified equipment, untrained staff, or jumpers who conceal health conditions. Neither of those should be an issue if you book through a vetted operator and are honest on your registration form.

## How to Choose an Operator You Can Trust

The jump itself takes four seconds. The quality of the operator governs everything else — the briefing, the harness fit, the cord inspection, and the emergency protocols.

When assessing an operator, look for:

- **Trained, certified jump masters** with documented training (international certifications from recognised bodies are the benchmark)
- **Documented equipment inspection logs** — cords, harnesses, and carabiners should be checked before each operating day and replaced on a manufacturer-recommended schedule
- **A proper briefing** — any operator who skips or rushes the safety briefing is a red flag
- **Clear medical and weight restrictions**, enforced without exception
- **Emergency protocols** — they should be able to tell you exactly what happens if something goes wrong

## What to Wear and Bring

Keep it simple:

- Comfortable, close-fitting clothes — nothing baggy that can catch or shift during the jump
- Trainers or sports shoes with a firm fit (some sites jump with shoes off for the ankle harness, but closed-toe footwear for the walk to the platform is important)
- Tie back long hair
- Remove glasses, contact lenses if possible, loose jewellery, and anything in your pockets
- Do not eat a heavy meal in the two hours before you jump

If you're jumping at Rishikesh in winter (November to February), bring a warm layer for waiting around — the gorge is cold in the shade. In summer, thin, breathable clothing works well.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is bungee jumping safe in India?

Yes, when you jump with a certified operator who uses rated equipment and employs trained jump masters. The risk increases sharply with unregulated setups, which do exist at informal tourist sites. Stick to established, documented operations with verifiable safety credentials, attend the full briefing, and be honest about your health on the registration form. No operator can guarantee your safety if you conceal a medical condition.

### How should I prepare mentally for the jump?

Most people find that the anxiety peaks in the few minutes before the jump, not during it. A few things help: trust the briefing and the harness, focus on the jump master's instructions rather than the drop, and commit to the jump when the count reaches zero. Hesitating on the platform extends the anxiety without reducing the jump. Many people describe the actual fall as peaceful rather than terrifying — it goes fast.

### Can I jump if I wear glasses or have a health condition?

Glasses and contact lenses are generally not recommended for the jump — the force of the rebound can dislodge them. Many operators loan goggles or allow the jump without them. For health conditions, read the operator's restrictions carefully and consult your doctor if you are in any doubt. Operators are within their rights to refuse a jump if they have safety concerns, and no reputable site will override a medical restriction.

### What is the best time of year to jump in Rishikesh?

October through February is generally the most comfortable period — weather is clear and cool. The monsoon (roughly July to September) brings heavy rain and the Ganges swells; some operators close or reduce operations during this period. April to June is hot but functional. If you're combining bungee with white-water rafting or other outdoor activities, October to early April is the sweet spot for conditions across all of them.

Rishikesh combines bungee with a full menu of adventure — rafting, cliff jumping, and kayaking are all available within a few kilometres of the jump site.
