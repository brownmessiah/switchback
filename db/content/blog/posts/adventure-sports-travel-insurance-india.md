Most adventure travellers in India buy a standard travel insurance policy, assume they are covered, and never read the fine print. Then something goes wrong — a twisted ankle on a Himalayan trail, a helicopter rescue from a remote valley, a cancelled rafting trip after a flash flood — and they discover the policy they paid for covers almost none of it. This post walks you through exactly what to look for, what to declare, and how to make sure your insurance actually matches what you plan to do.

## Why Standard Travel Insurance Often Fails Adventure Travellers

Insurance companies are in the business of measuring risk, and adventure activities carry risks that most standard travel policies are not priced to cover. Read any mid-range domestic travel policy carefully and you will almost certainly find a clause excluding "hazardous activities" or "adventure sports." That exclusion list typically includes white-water rafting, paragliding, scuba diving, bungee jumping, skiing, and high-altitude trekking.

The altitude exclusion is especially important to understand. Many policies cap trekking coverage at a stated altitude limit — commonly somewhere in the range of 3,500 to 4,500 metres — which rules out a significant number of [high-altitude treks](/activities/trekking) in the Indian Himalayas. Roopkund, Pin Parvati, the Goecha La approach to Kangchenjunga, and dozens of other popular routes cross above these thresholds. If you are planning any high-altitude trek and your policy has an altitude cap you have not checked, you may already be uninsured for the most consequential part of your trip.

## The Adventure Sports Rider: What It Is and Why You Need It

The practical solution for most travellers is an adventure sports rider — an add-on to an existing travel or health policy that explicitly brings adventure activities within the scope of cover. Some insurers also offer standalone activity-specific policies for a single trip or activity.

When you are buying or upgrading a policy, look for one that explicitly names the activity you plan to do. "Adventure sports" as a blanket category is better than nothing, but a policy that specifically lists rafting, paragliding, scuba diving, or trekking above [X] metres is far more useful — and far easier to claim against — than vague wording you have to argue about during an emergency.

Before you pay, confirm the rider covers the grade, depth, or altitude of the specific activity you are doing, not just the activity category in general.

## What Good Adventure Sports Cover Should Include

Not all adventure sports policies are built the same. Here is what you should be looking for, in order of importance:

- **Emergency medical treatment and hospitalisation.** This is the baseline. It should cover treatment at the nearest facility, even if that facility is a small district hospital in a remote area.
- **Emergency evacuation and helicopter rescue.** For any trek or activity in a remote mountain zone, this is arguably the most important line item. A helicopter rescue from above 4,000 metres in the Himalayas can cost anywhere from ₹1.5 lakh to several lakhs depending on distance, weather, and the type of aircraft required. Confirm the policy covers the full cost, not just a capped amount that falls well short of real-world costs.
- **Repatriation.** If you need to be moved to a better-equipped hospital, or returned home, your policy should cover it.
- **Trip cancellation and interruption.** Weather windows close suddenly in the mountains. River levels make rafting unsafe. A policy that covers non-refundable costs when an activity is called off due to conditions or a genuine emergency protects your investment, not just your body.
- **Gear and baggage.** Specialist gear — trekking poles, harnesses, wetsuits, dive equipment — is expensive. Check whether your policy covers loss, theft, or damage, and what the per-item cap is.

For scuba diving specifically, verify that the policy covers diving to your certified depth level. A recreational diver certified to 18 metres has different requirements than someone doing a deeper specialty dive, and some policies draw a line at recreational depths only.

## How to Declare and What to Carry

The single biggest reason adventure sport claims get rejected is non-disclosure. If you are doing any activity that could reasonably be described as hazardous, declare it when you buy the policy. Do not assume that because you did not mention paragliding, the insurer will pay out when you need emergency treatment after a landing incident.

Practically, this means:
1. Name every activity you plan to do, even if you are only doing it once.
2. Declare the altitude of any trek you are planning.
3. Note any pre-existing medical conditions, as these affect both eligibility and premiums.
4. Carry a printed or downloaded copy of your policy schedule, the policy number, and the 24-hour emergency helpline number — not just a screenshot buried in your email. Network connectivity in mountain areas is unreliable, and you may need to make a call or hand details to a local guide or hospital staff.

## For Domestic Indian Travellers vs International Visitors

If you are an Indian resident travelling within India, a domestic travel insurance policy with an adventure sports rider is the most straightforward option. Several Indian insurers offer these, and premiums for a week of covered activity are often quite reasonable — though you should compare policies rather than buying the cheapest option without reading the exclusions.

If you are an international visitor, your home country travel insurance may already include adventure sports cover, but you need to verify two things: that India is included in the geographic coverage, and that the specific activity and altitude are within scope. Do not rely on a general "worldwide cover" assumption without reading the activity exclusions.

In both cases, the guidance here is general. Insurance policies differ significantly across providers, and the only way to know exactly what you are covered for is to read your specific policy document before you travel.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does standard travel insurance cover white-water rafting in India?

Usually not without a specific adventure sports rider or add-on. Most standard policies list rafting under "hazardous activities" and exclude it from medical and evacuation cover. Check your policy's exclusion clause before you travel, and upgrade your cover if needed.

### Is there an altitude limit on trekking cover?

Yes — many policies cap trekking cover at between 3,500 and 4,500 metres. If your trek goes higher, you need a policy that explicitly covers your maximum altitude. Always declare the highest point you plan to reach when purchasing cover.

### What should I do in an emergency if I am in a remote area?

Call your insurer's 24-hour emergency helpline immediately — this is why carrying the number separately from your phone is important. Give your policy number, your location as precisely as possible, and describe the situation. For mountain rescues, the insurer will typically coordinate or reimburse an evacuation; do not wait to call assuming you will figure out payment later.

### Can I buy adventure sports insurance for a single activity or a single trip?

Yes. Some Indian insurers offer short-term or activity-specific policies in addition to annual riders. These can be cost-effective if you are doing one specific trip and do not want to upgrade a full annual policy. Compare the per-activity cost against an annual adventure rider if you travel more than once or twice a year.

The safest approach is to sort your insurance before you book your activity — not the night before you leave. Once you have cover that genuinely matches your plans, you can browse [activities and operators across India](/activities/trekking) and focus on the experience rather than the what-ifs.
