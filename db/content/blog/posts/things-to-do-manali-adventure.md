Manali sits at roughly 2,050 metres in the Kullu valley of Himachal Pradesh, and it earns its reputation as India's most versatile adventure base honestly. Whether you have three days or three weeks, this guide covers what to actually do, when to go, what it costs, and how to avoid the traps that catch first-time visitors.

## When to Go and What Each Season Gives You

The honest answer is that [Manali](/destinations/manali) has no bad season — only seasons that suit different activities.

**March to June** is the sweet spot for most people. The snow has retreated from the valley floor, wildflowers are out, the Beas is swollen and excellent for rafting, and the long-distance trekking routes open from late May onwards. Temperatures are comfortable — cool evenings, warm afternoons — and the crowds, while present, haven't peaked yet.

**October to February** swings the other way. Solang Valley gets reliable snow from December through February, making it the right window for skiing and snowboarding. Roads can be treacherous, and Rohtang is often closed, but Atal Tunnel keeps the Lahaul side accessible. If you love the cold and want the hills mostly to yourself, this is your season.

**July to September** is monsoon. Landslides are a genuine hazard on every road leading into and out of the valley. Several trekking routes become unsafe. If you're going anyway, check road conditions daily and carry extra flexibility in your plans.

## River Rafting on the Beas: Grades, Stretches, and Costs

The Beas through the Kullu-Manali corridor is one of the most accessible white-water experiences in India, and it does not require any prior rafting experience to enjoy.

The main commercial stretches run between **Pirdi and Jhiri**, and the shorter **Babeli section**. You'll encounter Grade II and Grade III rapids — enough to get genuinely wet and feel the current working against your paddle, but not so technical that a healthy adult needs to be anxious. The rafting season aligns tightly with the river's snowmelt: mid-April through June is prime, with the water fast and full. A short window reopens in September after the rains settle.

Approximate pricing runs from around **₹600 to ₹1,200 per person** for the standard Pirdi-to-Jhiri run, varying by operator, group size, and whether a full safety kit and guide are included in the quote. Always confirm the live price and exactly what is covered when you book — prices shift with season and demand.

What you should insist on before getting in the raft: a proper life jacket fitting (not just handed to you on the bank), a mandatory safety briefing, and a guide who covers what to do if you fall in. Any operator worth using will run through this without being asked.

## Paragliding and Snow Activities at Solang Valley

Solang Valley, roughly 14 km north of Manali town, is where most of the high-volume adventure activity is concentrated, and with good reason — the terrain is ideal.

**In summer**, [paragliding at Solang](/activities/paragliding) is the headline act. A tandem flight with a licensed pilot takes you off the slope and gives you a bird's-eye view of the Kullu valley and the surrounding peaks. Flights range from short ridge-soaring runs to longer glides depending on the lift conditions and the package you book. Approximate prices sit between **₹1,500 and ₹3,500** — the spread reflects flight duration and the altitude of your launch point. Confirm what you're getting before you pay.

A few practical notes: weight and age limits apply (typically you need to be between roughly 18–110 kg and in reasonable health; children require parental permission). Always fly with a pilot who holds a valid certification from the Aero Club of India or an equivalent authority. Don't fly if visibility is poor or the wind is picking up — a reputable operator will call off flights before you do.

**In winter**, the same valley becomes the skiing and snowboarding area. There are surface lifts, equipment rentals (skis, boots, jackets — factor the rental cost into your budget), and basic instruction available. It's not a European-grade ski resort, but it's a legitimate way to get your first turns or to enjoy a snow day if you're primarily a trekker or rafter who happens to visit in January.

Zorbing — rolling downhill in a large inflatable sphere — is also popular here and makes for a low-commitment, accessible bit of fun alongside the bigger activities.

## The Treks Around Manali Worth Your Time

The mountains above the Kullu valley hold some of the most satisfying high-altitude trekking in the Indian Himalaya. The three [treks around Manali](/adventure/trekking-in-manali) that consistently deliver are Hampta Pass, Beas Kund, and Bhrigu Lake.

**Hampta Pass (4,270 m)** is a 4–5 day crossing that takes you from the lush Kullu side over into the dry, lunar landscape of Lahaul. The contrast is striking enough that most trekkers describe it as experiencing two different planets back to back. It's suitable for fit beginners willing to spend a night at altitude. Season: late June to early September.

**Beas Kund (3,700 m)** is a 2–3 day return trek to the glacial lake that feeds the Beas river. It's shorter, lower, and more forgiving than Hampta — a good first high-altitude experience. The meadows at Dhundi and Bakarthach are genuinely beautiful.

**Bhrigu Lake (4,300 m)** can technically be done in 2–3 days with an early start, but three days is more comfortable. The lake sits in an open bowl and is often still partially frozen into June. The high camp views across the valley at dawn are among the best in the region.

For any of these routes, trekking independently without a local guide is possible but inadvisable if you don't have Himalayan navigation experience. Altitude sickness is real — ascend slowly, stay hydrated, and know the signs of AMS. Book with a local operator who includes a qualified guide and a clearly stated rescue protocol.

## Rohtang Pass, Atal Tunnel, and the Road to Lahaul

Rohtang Pass at 3,978 m is one of the most famous mountain passes in India, and the drive up is dramatic. However, there are things you need to know before you just hire a taxi and go.

An **online permit is mandatory** for all private vehicles heading to Rohtang. The permits are released a day or two in advance and sell out quickly in peak season — sort this the evening before, not the morning of. The pass is also closed on certain days each week (traditionally Tuesdays) to manage traffic, and it remains closed from roughly November through May due to snow.

The **Atal Tunnel**, opened in 2020, runs under the Rohtang range and connects Manali directly to Sissu in the Lahaul valley. At 9.02 km it's the world's longest highway tunnel above 10,000 feet. It means you can reach Lahaul year-round regardless of whether Rohtang is open — which changed the region completely. Sissu, just beyond the tunnel exit, is a quiet village with meadows and a waterfall that sees far fewer visitors than Manali and rewards a day trip.

## Manali Beyond the Adrenaline: Old Manali, Hadimba, and Vashisht

Not every hour in Manali needs to be spent at altitude. The town itself has texture worth spending time in.

**Old Manali**, across the Manalsu stream from the main bazaar, has a completely different character — narrower lanes, apple orchards, guest houses in converted family homes, and a cluster of good cafes. It's unhurried and genuinely pleasant for an evening walk.

**Hadimba Devi Temple**, built in 1553, sits in a cedar forest at the edge of town. The four-storey wooden shikhara is architecturally unusual and the walk through the forest feels calming in a way the main bazaar does not.

**Vashisht village**, 3 km from the town centre, has natural hot sulphur springs. There are separate public bathing tanks (modest fee) and the village has a working community feel that the more tourist-facing parts of Manali have lost.

## How to Pick a Safe, Reliable Operator

Manali has a large and uneven adventure operator market. This matters because the same activity — paragliding, rafting, trekking — can range from well-run and genuinely safe to shoddily managed with second-rate equipment.

- **Check verification.** Look for operators who can show you instructor certification, equipment maintenance records, and insurance. KYC-verified operators on platforms like Switchback have been checked against these standards.
- **Read the briefing seriously.** A rushed or skipped safety briefing is a red flag, not a time-saver.
- **Know the weight, age, and health limits.** Every reputable operator has them. If someone waves them away to close a sale, walk away.
- **Ask what happens if conditions are bad.** The answer should be: the activity is called off and you're rescheduled or refunded. Free cancellation within policy is a baseline expectation, not a luxury.
- **Don't anchor on price alone.** The ₹200 saving on rafting is not worth a substandard life jacket.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need prior experience for any of these activities?

For river rafting on the Beas (Pirdi-Babeli stretch) and tandem paragliding at Solang, no prior experience is needed — you're with a trained guide throughout. Trekking to Beas Kund or Bhrigu Lake requires reasonable fitness but no technical skill. Hampta Pass demands better fitness and ideally some hill-walking background. Skiing requires instruction if you're a beginner, which local operators at Solang provide.

### Is the Rohtang Pass permit complicated to arrange?

The permit is applied for online through the Himachal Pradesh government portal. You'll need your vehicle registration number and identification. Permits are released a day or two before the date, and popular travel days fill up quickly — check the evening before rather than the morning you plan to go. Shared cabs and local tour operators in Manali often handle the permit as part of a day-trip package, which simplifies it considerably.

### What is the best base for doing multiple activities in one trip?

Manali town or Old Manali puts you within 15–30 minutes of Solang Valley, the Beas rafting put-in points, and the trailheads for Beas Kund and the Hampta Pass approach. For Bhrigu Lake the trailhead is near Vashisht. You do not need to change accommodation between most activities — a single base in the town works for a 4–7 day trip covering the full range.

### Are these activities safe for children or older adults?

Age and health eligibility vary by activity and operator. Paragliding typically requires a minimum age of around 14–16 and an upper weight limit; most operators will not take children under 12. Rafting has similar age minimums and is not recommended for anyone with serious cardiovascular conditions. Trekking is broadly accessible but altitude (even moderate Himalayan altitudes above 3,000 m) affects people unpredictably — go slowly, don't push through headaches, and be honest about fitness levels before choosing a route. Always check the specific operator's stated limits before booking.
