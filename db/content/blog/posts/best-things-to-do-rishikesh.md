Rishikesh earns its reputation. Tucked into the foothills where the Ganga emerges from the Himalayas, it delivers everything from heart-in-mouth rapids to quiet meditation halls within a few square kilometres. This guide covers 12 of the best things to do in [Rishikesh](/destinations/rishikesh) — what each experience actually involves, roughly what it costs, who it suits, and the honest caveats you need before you book.

## 1. White-Water Rafting on the Ganga

[White-water rafting](/adventure/rafting-in-rishikesh) is the single most popular reason people make the trip, and it deserves the hype. The Ganga between Shivpuri, Marine Drive, and Brahmpuri offers rapids ranging from Grade I (gentle riffles, fine for first-timers) to Grade IV (powerful, technical stretches like the notorious Roller Coaster and Golf Course rapids). Most operators run half-day trips of 9–16 km; full-day expeditions of 26–36 km are available for those who want a longer stretch.

Approximate prices range from around ₹600 for a short beginner stretch to ₹2,500 or more for a longer, more challenging run — confirm the live price directly with your operator at the time of booking, as it shifts seasonally. You must be able to swim or wear a life jacket competently. Most operators set a minimum age of around 14 and a weight ceiling around 120 kg; check with your specific operator. The briefing before you launch is not a formality — pay attention to it, especially paddle commands and what to do if you fall in.

## 2. Bungee Jumping at Mohanchatti

At roughly 83 metres above a rocky gorge, the bungee platform at Mohanchatti is one of the highest fixed-platform bungee jumps in India. The free fall lasts about three seconds and the rebound is long and elastic. It is not a mild thrill.

[Bungee jumping](/activities/bungee-jumping) in Rishikesh typically costs in the range of ₹3,500–₹4,000; confirm the current rate when you book. Operators generally require jumpers to be at least 12 years old, weigh between roughly 40 kg and 110 kg, and be free of back, neck, or heart conditions. Pregnant women and anyone with serious joint or spinal issues should not jump. If you are on the fence about your health eligibility, check with a doctor before you travel, not at the platform.

## 3. The Giant Swing and Flying Fox

If a full bungee feels like too much commitment, the giant swing offers a different kind of adrenaline. You are harnessed into a seat, lifted to height, and released into a long pendulum arc over the valley — the feeling is closer to a roller coaster than a free fall. Prices are usually lower than bungee; expect approximately ₹1,500–₹2,500, though rates vary by operator and season.

The flying fox (zip line) at similar locations runs you along a steel cable over a forested gorge. Both activities are typically run by the same outfitters offering bungee, and many operators bundle all three into a package that works out cheaper than booking separately.

## 4. Cliff Jumping and Kayaking

Several spots along the river offer cliff-jumping platforms of varying heights — roughly 5 metres to 25 metres — accessible on foot or combined with a rafting trip. The jump itself is simple but the current below is not always calm; always assess conditions with your guide before you leap and never jump into unfamiliar water without a local confirming it is safe that day.

For something quieter but technically engaging, kayaking on calmer stretches of the Ganga rewards patience and upper-body effort. Introductory sessions are widely available and take around two to three hours. More experienced paddlers can attempt longer kayak tours. Both activities suit the September–November and February–June windows.

## 5. Riverside Camping Under the Stars

Staying in a riverside camp — tents pitched on sandy banks a few kilometres upstream from town, away from road noise — is one of the best ways to slow Rishikesh down. Most camps sit between Shivpuri and Marine Drive and combine an overnight stay with a morning or afternoon rafting slot.

Facilities range from basic (shared bathrooms, basic meals) to reasonably comfortable (attached toilet tents, bonfire evenings, multi-cuisine meals). Prices vary widely by standard and season; budget camps may be available from around ₹1,500 per person including meals and a rafting stretch, while more comfortable setups run higher. Confirm exactly what is included before you pay. October and early November, when the air is cool and skies are clear, are particularly good months for camping.

## 6. Trekking to Neer Garh Waterfall and Beyond

You do not need to be a serious trekker to enjoy the trails around Rishikesh. The walk to Neer Garh Waterfall is one of the most accessible — a gentle 2 km trail through forest that ends at a two-tiered fall with a natural pool. It is a good option if you want green hills without altitude. The trail is reasonably well-marked and takes about 45 minutes each way at a relaxed pace.

For those with more time, the trail toward Kunjapuri temple (a pre-dawn climb for sunrise views) and the paths into the foothills above the town are worth exploring. Footwear with grip matters more than anything else on these trails; flip-flops are a reliable way to twist an ankle.

## 7. Lakshman Jhula and Ram Jhula

Walking across these two famous suspension bridges is a Rishikesh rite of passage. Lakshman Jhula, the older of the two, connects the neighbourhoods of Tapovan and Jonk across the Ganga. Ram Jhula links Sivananda Ashram to Swarg Ashram. Both offer views up and down the river that are worth the short walk, especially in the early morning before the crowds arrive.

The bridges are also useful orientation points for exploring the ghats, the temple-lined alleys of Swarg Ashram, and the cluster of cafes and bookshops that have accumulated on both banks over the decades.

## 8. The Beatles Ashram (Chaurasi Kutia)

In 1968, the Beatles spent several weeks studying Transcendental Meditation at the ashram of Maharishi Mahesh Yogi in Rishikesh, now formally known as Chaurasi Kutia and maintained by the Forest Department. The ashram fell into disuse for decades, making it one of the most atmospheric ruins in Uttarakhand — buildings swallowed by creeper vines, meditation domes, and the occasional piece of surviving painted wall art.

A small entry fee applies (confirm the current rate at the gate). It takes about an hour to walk the grounds properly. The silence and overgrowth make it genuinely worth visiting regardless of any interest in the Beatles themselves.

## 9. Evening Ganga Aarti at Parmarth Niketan

Every evening at Parmarth Niketan — and at Triveni Ghat — priests perform the Ganga Aarti: a rhythmic, lamp-lit ceremony on the riverside steps that draws both pilgrims and visitors. It runs for about 45 minutes and is free to attend.

The Parmarth Niketan aarti is larger and more produced; the one at Triveni Ghat is somewhat smaller and a little easier to approach on foot. Either is worth attending at least once. Go 20 minutes early to find a good position on the ghats. Photography is generally permitted but read the room — this is an active religious ceremony, not a performance.

## 10. Yoga, Meditation, and Wellness Retreats

Rishikesh holds a genuine claim to being a global hub for yoga — the Himalayan tradition of practice here runs deep, and the serious ashrams have been teaching for generations. Options range from a single 90-minute drop-in class (approximately ₹300–₹700 at many centres) to multi-day residential courses at established ashrams.

If you want a substantive experience rather than a box to tick, book a retreat of at least three to five days and check the credentials of the teacher. Sivananda Ashram, Parmarth Niketan, and several independent schools have well-established programmes. Rishikesh also attracts many newer wellness centres; quality varies, so read recent, specific reviews rather than relying on overall star ratings.

## 11. Cafe-Hopping on the Ganga Banks

The cafe culture on both sides of the river is eclectic and genuinely good. You will find proper espresso, Israeli breakfasts, South Indian dosas, wood-fired pizza, and Tibetan thukpa within a five-minute walk of one another in the Laxman Jhula and Swarg Ashram areas. Prices are very reasonable by any metric — a good meal with chai rarely exceeds ₹400–₹600.

Several rooftop cafes on the north bank offer direct river views that make for a slow, restorative afternoon between activities. This is not filler — after a day of rafting or a long trek, an hour with a book and good coffee is exactly what the body needs.

## 12. Shoulder-Season Planning and How to Get There

The best months for adventure activities are **September to November** and **February to June**. The Ganga runs high and fast (and more dangerous) during peak monsoon from late June through August; many rafting operators pause operations in July and August or restrict to calmer stretches. Winter (December–January) is cold but peaceful and better suited to yoga retreats than water sports.

Rishikesh is reachable via Jolly Grant Airport in Dehradun (roughly 20 km away, with regular flights from Delhi, Mumbai, and Bengaluru) or by train to Haridwar (well-connected to the national rail network), followed by a road transfer of about 25 km. From Delhi by road, the drive is around 250 km via NH 58; budget 5–7 hours depending on traffic.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is white-water rafting in Rishikesh safe for beginners?

Yes, for the Grade I–II stretches. The beginner runs near Brahmpuri and Shivpuri are genuinely manageable for most adults who can follow basic instructions, even if you have never rafted before. Choose a KYC-verified operator, attend the pre-launch safety briefing fully, and be honest about your swimming ability when you book. Avoid the longer Grade III–IV routes if it is your first time on moving water.

### What is the minimum age for bungee jumping in Rishikesh?

Most operators set the minimum at around 12 years old, with a minimum weight of roughly 40 kg and a maximum of around 110 kg. Age and weight limits can vary between operators, so confirm before you travel. Anyone with a history of back problems, neck injuries, heart conditions, or epilepsy should consult a doctor first and disclose their condition to the operator.

### How many days should I plan for Rishikesh?

Three to four days covers the adventure highlights — a rafting day, the jump activities, an aarti, the ashram walk, and Lakshman Jhula — without feeling rushed. A week allows you to add a yoga course, a longer trek, and the slower pleasures of the cafes and ghats. If you are coming purely for a multi-day yoga retreat, a week to ten days is more appropriate.

### When should I avoid visiting Rishikesh?

Mid-July to late August is peak monsoon. The Ganga runs dangerously high, many adventure operators suspend water activities, landslides can close roads, and the weather is persistently wet. It is not impossible to visit, but most of the activities in this guide will not be available or will be significantly curtailed. The shoulder periods just before and after monsoon — September through mid-October and late February through June — are far more rewarding.
