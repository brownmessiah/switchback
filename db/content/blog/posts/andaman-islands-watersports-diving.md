The Andaman Islands sit in the Bay of Bengal with water so clear you can watch your own shadow on the seabed 20 metres below. If you have been wondering whether the diving and watersports here live up to the hype, the honest answer is: for India, nothing else comes close. This guide covers the best spots, what each activity actually involves, what you will spend, and what you need to know before you go.

## Why the Andamans Are India's Top Watersports Destination

Most of India's coastline deals with silt, boat traffic, or seasonal algae. The Andaman archipelago is different. The islands are remote enough that the reefs have not been overfished or bleached into rubble, and the lack of major rivers means sediment run-off is minimal. Underwater visibility commonly sits between 15 and 30 metres, which puts these waters in the same conversation as Southeast Asian dive destinations.

The main hubs for watersports are Havelock Island (officially Swaraj Dweep) and Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep). Port Blair on South Andaman is your gateway — it has the only commercial airport — but most travellers spend as little time there as necessary before catching a ferry east.

## When to Visit: Seasons and Sea Conditions

The best window for [Andaman watersports](/destinations/andaman) is October to May. Within that window, the water is calmest and visibility is sharpest from November through April. December and January bring a slight nip in the air at night — pleasant for beach evenings — while March and April are warm and the sea is glassy on most days.

May is the shoulder month: operators are still running trips, but you may encounter pre-monsoon swells towards the end of the month. June through September is monsoon season. The sea gets rough, ferries are disrupted, and most dive centres scale back or shut entirely. Travelling during the monsoon is not impossible, but it requires flexibility and the understanding that your dive or snorkel trip may be cancelled at short notice.

If your dates are fixed to the monsoon window, plan an onshore itinerary as a genuine backup — Port Blair has the Cellular Jail and the Anthropological Museum — rather than counting on water activities.

## Scuba Diving: From First Timers to Advanced Certifications

[Scuba diving](/activities/scuba-diving) is the reason most watersports travellers make the journey. The reefs around Havelock, particularly sites like Elephant Beach and Nemo Reef, host hard and soft corals alongside sea turtles, reef sharks, barracuda, and enormous schools of anthias. Visibility at many sites reaches 20 metres or more on a clear day.

**Discover Scuba Diving (DSD)** is the right starting point if you have never dived before. An instructor accompanies you throughout, covers basic equipment use and equalisation on the surface, and then guides you to a depth of roughly 5–6 metres. No prior certification or swimming proficiency is required, though you should be comfortable in open water. Budget approximately **₹3,500 to ₹5,500** per person for a DSD session, depending on the operator and the site. Confirm the live price at the time of booking — fuel costs and equipment maintenance mean rates do shift seasonally.

Certified divers have a wider choice of sites and can go deeper. Popular options include fun dives (2 dives per day, with a dive master guiding small groups) and multi-day PADI Open Water, Advanced, and Rescue Diver courses. The Andamans are a genuinely good place to do your Open Water certification because the conditions are benign and the marine life provides strong motivation during skills practice.

**On safety:** always choose a centre with certified instructors and properly maintained equipment. Switchback lists KYC-verified dive operators, which means the business and lead instructor documentation have been checked — this matters because not every hut on the beach offering "cheap diving" meets that standard. Attend the full briefing, disclose any health conditions, and respect the site's depth limits. The standard rule applies everywhere: do not fly within 18 to 24 hours of your last dive.

**Reef etiquette:** the health of these reefs is not accidental — it is the result of low overall visitor numbers relative to places like Phuket or the Maldives. Do not touch corals, do not stand on the reef to adjust your gear, and use reef-safe sunscreen. Every diver who respects this makes the next diver's experience better.

## Snorkelling: Accessible and Genuinely Worthwhile

If you are not ready for scuba, snorkelling at Elephant Beach (Havelock) and Sitapur Beach (Neil) offers healthy shallow reefs at depths of 1 to 5 metres. You will see parrotfish, clownfish, sea urchins, and — if you are patient and quiet — the occasional turtle.

Most day trips to Elephant Beach include a basic snorkel kit in the price. Expect to pay roughly **₹600 to ₹1,200** for a combined boat ride and snorkel package, though independent snorkelling from the beach itself is free if you bring your own mask and fins. Operators running guided snorkel sessions can point out the best coral patches and keep the group together, which is worth the marginal cost for first-timers.

## Sea Kayaking: Mangroves and Bioluminescence

Sea kayaking through the mangrove creeks around Havelock is underrated. The paddle routes wind through dense roots and let you drift alongside kingfishers and mud skippers in near silence. Sit-on-top kayaks are the standard craft — stable, forgiving, and suitable for people with no prior kayaking experience.

The stand-out offering, if you visit between November and April, is the **bioluminescence night kayak**. The micro-organisms in certain sheltered creeks emit a cold blue-green light when disturbed, so every paddle stroke and hand-trail leaves a trail of glow. It sounds gimmicky; in person it is quietly extraordinary. Group sizes are small by necessity — typically 4 to 8 people — so book ahead, especially around the December–January peak. Prices run approximately **₹1,500 to ₹2,500** per person.

## Glass-Bottom Boats and Game Fishing

For travellers who want to see the reef without getting wet, glass-bottom boat rides operate out of most beaches. They cover the shallow reefs at a gentle pace and are a reasonable option for young children or anyone not comfortable in the water. Expect to pay **₹500 to ₹800** for a 30–45 minute session.

Game fishing in the Andamans targets species like wahoo, giant trevally, and queenfish. Trips typically run 4 to 6 hours and go well offshore. This is a niche activity with a small number of operators; pricing is higher than other watersports — roughly **₹6,000 to ₹15,000** per boat for a half-day charter, shared across the group. Confirm availability and exact rates directly with the operator.

## Getting There and Moving Between Islands

Flights land at Veer Savarkar International Airport in Port Blair. Direct flights operate from Chennai, Kolkata, Delhi, Mumbai, and a few other cities. Book early: seats fill up during peak season (December to February) and fares rise sharply.

From Port Blair, government ferries and private speedboats connect to Havelock and Neil. The government ferry takes roughly 90 minutes to Havelock on the Makruzz or Green Ocean services; a basic vessel takes longer. Book ferry tickets in advance during peak season — they sell out. Carrying sea-sickness medication is sensible if you are prone; the crossing can be choppy.

Budget at minimum 3 nights on Havelock to fit in diving and a couple of day activities without feeling rushed. Neil is smaller and quieter; a 2-night add-on works well if you want a slower pace and different snorkel sites.

## What to Pack for Andaman Watersports

- **Reef-safe, mineral sunscreen** — standard chemical sunscreens harm coral; many operators now ask for this specifically
- **Rash guard or light wetsuit top** — sun protection during long snorkel or kayak sessions; water temperature sits around 27–29°C year-round so you do not need thermal exposure protection
- **Waterproof dry bag** — for phone, cash, and documentation on boat trips
- **Any dive certification card** — your PADI, SSI, or equivalent card; a digital version on your phone is acceptable at most centres
- **Prescription disclosure** — if you take medication that could interact with diving (certain antihistamines, blood pressure medication), check with your GP before the trip; many conditions require clearance, not prohibition

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to know how to swim to try scuba diving or snorkelling?

For Discover Scuba Diving, most operators require basic comfort in water rather than formal swimming ability — you are accompanied by an instructor at all times. For snorkelling, a life jacket is available and you can snorkel in shallow water. That said, being a confident swimmer makes every water activity safer and more enjoyable. If you are not, mention it when booking so the operator can assign the right instructor and site.

### Is it safe to dive in the Andamans as a complete beginner?

Yes, provided you choose a certified operator. The conditions at beginner sites like Elephant Beach are gentle — low current, warm water, and relatively shallow depths. The risks exist, as with any diving, but they are well managed by qualified instructors. The briefing matters: pay attention to equalisation technique and what to do if you feel uncomfortable underwater. Never skip it.

### How far in advance should I book dive courses and night kayaking?

For December through February, book at least 2 to 3 weeks ahead for PADI courses (they fill by certified group size) and night kayaking (small group limits). For DSD and fun dives, a few days' notice is usually sufficient outside of peak week (Christmas–New Year), when everything is tight. During October–November and March–May, same-day or next-day bookings are often possible.

### Are there age or health restrictions for watersports?

Most operators set a minimum age of 10–12 years for DSD and a minimum age of 8 for snorkelling. Weight and medical limits apply to diving: active asthma, recent ear surgery, and certain heart conditions can disqualify you, and operators are required to ask. Kayaking has the fewest restrictions and is appropriate for most fitness levels. Always disclose health conditions when booking — it protects you, not just the operator.
