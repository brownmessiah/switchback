Standing 83 metres above a rocky gorge on a cantilever platform, you will almost certainly question every decision that brought you here. This guide gives you an honest, practical answer to whether bungee jumping in [Rishikesh](/destinations/rishikesh) is worth your time and money — covering the jump site, what the experience actually feels like, who it suits (and who it does not), costs, and how to book smart.

## The Jump Site: Mohanchatti, Not the Town

The main bungee site in Rishikesh is not on the famous ghats. It sits at Mohanchatti, roughly 20–25 km from Rishikesh town, in a forested gorge above the Shivalik foothills. The platform is a fixed cantilever structure — meaning it does not sway or float like a crane-based setup — at approximately 83 metres above the valley floor. That height has made it India's highest fixed-platform bungee jump for years.

The same site also operates a giant swing and a flying fox, which means you can turn this into a half-day of aerial activity rather than a single 5-second moment.

Getting there takes 30–45 minutes by road from Rishikesh. Most operators offer pick-up packages, or you can hire a local taxi or auto. Allow a full morning or afternoon — the weigh-in, briefing, gearing up, and waiting for your turn all take time.

## What Actually Happens When You Jump

Here is the sequence, stripped of marketing language. You arrive, fill in a waiver form, and step on a scale. The weight check is not a formality — the bungee cord is calibrated to your body weight, and jumping outside the approved range (roughly 40–110 kg at this site) is a genuine safety concern, not a bureaucratic hurdle.

Once you clear the weigh-in, jump masters fit you into a full-body harness and ankle attachment. You get a safety briefing that covers the jump position, head placement, and what to do during the rebound. Pay attention. This briefing is the most important 10 minutes of your visit.

Then the walk to the edge. The platform extends over the gorge and the drop is fully visible. Most people describe this part — not the jump itself — as the hardest. Your rational brain argues loudly. The countdown begins, and then you are in free fall.

The fall itself lasts a few seconds. There is a rebound, then a slower oscillation as the cord does its job. A rope is lowered to bring you back up, or in some configurations you are guided down to a mat below. From jump to feet-on-ground, it is intense, short, and — for most people who do it — profoundly satisfying.

## What It Costs and What Is Included

Pricing at the Mohanchatti site typically runs in the range of **approximately ₹3,500 to ₹4,000** for the jump itself. Video of your jump is usually an add-on and costs extra — worth considering, since the moment goes by too fast to photograph yourself.

The giant swing is priced separately and often gives you a longer sensation of free fall than the bungee itself, because the arc travels further before the cord pulls. If you are debating whether the bungee is worth it alone, pairing it with the swing meaningfully extends the experience for a modest additional cost.

Always confirm the current price directly with the operator when you [book a bungee jump](/activities/bungee-jumping) — prices can shift with season, advance-booking offers, or group packages. Treat any figure you read online, including here, as an orientation, not a commitment.

## Who Should Jump — and Who Should Not

Bungee jumping is not for everyone, and an honest guide has to say that plainly.

**You are a strong candidate if:**
- You are between roughly 40 and 110 kg body weight
- You are at least around 12 years old (minors need guardian consent)
- You have no active heart, back, neck, or spinal conditions
- You are not pregnant
- You have not had recent surgery
- You want the specific sensation of free fall and are prepared for real adrenaline, not a theme-park simulation

**You should sit this one out if:**
- You have hypertension, a heart condition, or a history of seizures
- You have a back or neck injury — the rebound places real stress on the spine
- You are pregnant
- You had surgery recently and have not cleared strenuous activity with a doctor
- The height or activity genuinely fills you with dread rather than excitement — this is a legitimate reason, and there are plenty of other things to do in Rishikesh

If you are on the edge about a health condition, get a doctor's sign-off before you book. The operators will ask.

## Practical Tips to Make the Day Go Smoothly

**Book in advance.** Slots at the main site fill up, especially on weekends, during peak season (October to June), and around public holidays. Walk-in availability in busy periods is unreliable.

**Go early.** Morning slots tend to have cooler temperatures and shorter queues. Afternoons in summer can be hot, and waiting in the sun for your turn is less pleasant than it sounds.

**Wear the right clothes.** Comfortable, close-fitting clothes and sports shoes. Loose jewellery, wallets, and phones need to stay secured with your group — there are no pockets in a harness and no retrieval if something falls 83 metres.

**Eat light beforehand.** A full meal 1–2 hours before is fine. A heavy lunch 20 minutes before is not.

**Attend the entire safety brief.** Jump masters are trained specifically for this site. Their instructions are site-specific, not generic, and the head and neck positioning guidance exists for a reason.

**Pair activities thoughtfully.** The giant swing at the same site genuinely extends the free-fall sensation. Many visitors find it even more exhilarating than the bungee because the arc is longer. The flying fox is a gentler intro if you want to ease into the height.

## Choosing a Certified Operator

Rishikesh has a strong outdoor adventure culture and serious operators, but not everyone at every site has the same standards. Look for operators who can demonstrate equipment certification, trained jump masters with visible credentials, and clear safety protocols. On platforms like Switchback, operators go through KYC verification, which gives you a baseline assurance that you are dealing with a registered, identifiable business — not someone who set up a swing over a gorge last week.

Ask whether their cord and harness equipment is regularly inspected. A good operator will answer this without hesitation. One who deflects or gets vague is a signal to look elsewhere.

Transparent pricing — knowing exactly what is and is not included before you pay — is another mark of a trustworthy setup. Get clarity on what the base price covers, what the video costs, and what the cancellation or rescheduling policy is before you commit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is bungee jumping in Rishikesh safe?

No adventure activity is without risk, and anyone who tells you otherwise is not being straight with you. The Mohanchatti site has been operating for years with trained jump masters, certified equipment, and established protocols. Your job is to attend the full safety briefing, disclose any relevant health conditions honestly, and follow the jump master's instructions exactly. Choose a verified operator, not just the cheapest listing you find.

### What is the best time of year to go?

The site typically operates from October through June. The monsoon season (roughly July to September) brings heavy rain and high water levels that can close outdoor activity sites. October to February offers cooler, cleaner conditions; March to June is warmer but generally fine. Always check with the operator about current conditions and closures before travelling.

### Can I bring my phone or camera on the platform?

No. Loose items on the platform are a safety hazard and a guaranteed loss if they fall. Most sites allow a friend or family member to watch from a designated area. The operator-provided video package is the practical way to have a record of your jump — and it is shot by someone who does this every day and knows the angle.

### I am nervous. Should I still go?

Nervousness is completely normal and does not mean you should skip it. The people who say bungee jumping changed something for them are almost always describing the moment of stepping off despite being afraid. That said, there is a difference between healthy nerves and genuine dread. If your gut is saying no for a health reason or because the idea brings you genuine distress rather than excited anticipation, trust that. Rishikesh has river rafting, cliff jumping, kayaking, and dozens of other ways to earn an adrenaline story.

The bottom line is straightforward: if you want to know what free fall feels like, the Mohanchatti jump is a well-established, honestly run experience that delivers exactly what it promises. Pair it with the giant swing, book your slot in advance, and explore everything else [Rishikesh](/destinations/rishikesh) has to offer around it — the town rewards an extra day or two of exploration beyond the jump itself.
