Bir Billing sits quietly in the Kangra Valley of Himachal Pradesh, yet it carries a reputation that reaches every corner of the adventure travel world. This guide covers everything you need to plan a real trip — when to go, how to get there, what paragliding actually costs, where to sleep, and what to do on the ground when you are not in the air.

## Why Bir Billing Is India's Paragliding Capital

The geography here is almost absurdly good for flying. Billing, the take-off point, sits at roughly 2,400 metres above sea level. Bir, the landing zone, lies about a thousand metres lower at around 1,400 metres. That vertical drop, combined with the reliable thermal activity that builds over the Dhauladhar ranges each morning, gives pilots and tandem passengers a long, soaring glide that few sites in Asia can match.

The site's credibility is not just geography. Bir Billing hosted the Paragliding World Cup in 2015, which put it firmly on the map for the global flying community. Since then, licensed schools and certified tandem pilots have established themselves here in serious numbers. For a first-time flyer who wants to experience [paragliding in Bir Billing](/adventure/paragliding-in-bir-billing) with a qualified professional, the infrastructure — briefings, safety gear, rescue protocols — is genuinely developed compared with many adventure sites in India.

## When to Go: Seasons That Actually Deliver

Two windows are considered ideal, and experienced visitors tend to pick one or the other with clear reasoning.

**October to November** is arguably the sweeter of the two. The monsoon has cleared, skies are transparent, the valley is green but not wet, and temperatures are mild enough to be comfortable at Billing without heavy gear. Thermals are strong and consistent, and visibility can extend to snow-capped peaks on still mornings. This window fills up, so plan accommodation at least a few weeks ahead.

**March to June** is the spring and early-summer season. Flowers are out on the hillsides, the Tibetan colony is lively with visitors, and weather is warm in Bir while Billing stays fresh. Flying is excellent through April and May. June can get hazy and the pre-monsoon instability creeps in toward the end of the month.

**Avoid the monsoon (roughly July to mid-September).** Most reputable operators close tandem flights entirely during this period. Thermals become unpredictable, cloud base drops, and conditions turn unsafe. If you arrive during the monsoon expecting to fly, you are likely to be disappointed.

## Getting to Bir Billing: Routes and Realities

Bir is not on the main tourist highway, which is part of its charm, but it does require a deliberate journey.

**By air:** The nearest airport is Gaggal (Kangra Airport), roughly 70 kilometres from Bir. From there, hire a cab — the drive takes around 90 minutes to two hours depending on traffic and road conditions. Fares vary; confirm with your driver before departing.

**From Dharamshala or McLeodganj:** This is the most common approach for travellers already in the region. Bir is approximately four to five hours by road. Shared cabs and buses run this route, but a private cab gives you flexibility, especially if you are carrying gear or arriving late.

**By train:** The nearest broad-gauge railhead is Pathankot, from where you take road transport north into the hills. Some travellers use the narrow-gauge Kangra Valley Railway to Ahju station, which is a few kilometres from Bir — a scenic option if you are not in a hurry.

**By road from Delhi:** A direct overnight bus from Delhi to Bir runs on some services; otherwise, connect via Pathankot or Dharamshala. Budget roughly 12–14 hours for the full journey.

## What Tandem Paragliding Costs — and What to Watch For

For a tandem flight — where you fly strapped to a certified pilot who handles everything — expect to pay approximately **₹2,000 to ₹3,500**. Prices sit at the lower end during off-peak periods and rise in the October–November and spring peak windows. Always confirm the current price directly with the operator at the time of booking, since seasonal demand and fuel costs for the vehicle that drives you up to Billing shift rates from year to year.

The standard package typically includes the jeep ride up to Billing, the flight itself (usually 15–30 minutes depending on conditions and your pilot's read of the thermals), and the landing at the Bir landing field. GoPro video footage is often offered as an add-on; prices for this vary and are worth asking about upfront so there is no surprise at the end.

A few practical checkpoints:
- Confirm your pilot holds a valid BHPA or APPI certification, or the equivalent Indian licence
- Weight limits typically apply — operators generally have a ceiling of around 100–110 kg; confirm before you book if this is relevant for you
- Age minimums are usually set around 5–7 years; no hard upper limit but a reasonable level of fitness is expected
- Attend the pre-flight briefing properly — it is short and genuinely important
- Pregnant travellers and those with recent bone injuries or heart conditions should consult a doctor and be honest with the operator

Choosing a KYC-verified operator matters here. At [Bir Billing](/destinations/bir-billing), the most visible operators are not always the most rigorous. Booking through a platform that vets operators for legitimate certification reduces the chance of cutting corners on equipment or pilot qualifications.

## Beyond the Sky: What Else to Do in Bir

Bir is a Tibetan settlement — one of the more significant ones in Himachal Pradesh — and that cultural layer is genuinely worth spending time with.

**Chokling Monastery and Sherabling Monastery** are the two main monastic institutions. Sherabling in particular is a working monastery with a large residential community of monks; the main prayer hall is open to respectful visitors and the grounds are peaceful in the early morning. Neither requires a guide — you can walk to both from central Bir on foot.

**The market and cafes in Bir village** have developed considerably over the past decade. German bakeries, cafes serving good espresso, thali joints, and Tibetan restaurants all sit within a short walk of each other. The food quality is higher than you might expect for a village this size.

**Short hikes** are available directly from Bir. Trails into the cedar and deodar forests above the village take between one and three hours at an easy pace and give you elevated views without requiring any preparation or guides.

**The Billing-to-Bir trek** reverses the paragliding route on foot. It is not a technical trek — the path is wide and well-marked — but the altitude gain makes it a solid half-day effort with good payoff in views. Many visitors who fly one day do this trek the next.

**Mountain biking** is possible on the roads and trails around Bir. A handful of outfitters in the village rent bikes; the terrain rewards riders comfortable with hills.

**[Camping under the stars](/activities/camping)** in the meadows around Billing is a different kind of experience entirely. Sleeping at altitude with the Dhauladhars above you and the valley spread below is one of the better quiet decisions you can make on this trip. Organised camps at Billing operate in peak season; confirm availability and what is included (meals, sleeping gear) before you commit.

## Where to Stay: Bir Village vs Billing

This is a practical decision worth thinking through before you arrive.

**Bir village** has the most accommodation variety — guesthouses, homestays, small hotels, and a growing number of boutique options. It is warmer, has better food access, and puts you within walking distance of the monasteries and cafes. If you are spending more than one or two nights, staying in Bir gives you more to do after dark.

**Billing** has a small number of camps and basic guesthouses. Staying up top means you wake at altitude, see the morning thermals develop, and can walk to the launch site without a jeep. It suits pilots doing extended courses more than casual visitors. It is colder, and food options are limited to what the camps provide.

Most first-time visitors base themselves in Bir village and jeep up to Billing on flying day.

## A Suggested 2-Day Itinerary

**Day 1**
Arrive in Bir by midday. Check in, eat lunch at one of the Tibetan restaurants near the market. Walk to Chokling or Sherabling Monastery in the afternoon — give yourself 90 minutes. Return to the village, have dinner, go to bed reasonably early since the best flying starts mid-morning and you want to be fresh.

**Day 2**
Jeep up to Billing before 10am. Attend your pre-flight briefing, check in with your pilot, and fly. The descent takes you back to Bir's landing field; the whole morning is usually done by noon. Spend the afternoon on the Billing-to-Bir trail if you have the energy, or explore the village, pick up some Tibetan handicrafts, and have an unhurried lunch before heading out.

If you have a third day, add camping at Billing or a longer forest walk out of the village.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is paragliding at Bir Billing safe for first-timers?

Tandem paragliding with a certified pilot is designed for people with no flying experience. The pilot handles all controls; your job is to run a few steps during take-off, sit in the harness, and enjoy the flight. Safety depends heavily on the operator you choose — verify certifications, attend the briefing fully, and be honest about any medical conditions. Weather closures are a genuine possibility; do not book a non-refundable onward journey immediately after a planned flying day.

### What should I wear and pack for the flight?

Wear comfortable, close-fitting layers — nothing loose that can catch wind. Closed-toe shoes are essential; sandals are refused at most launch sites. Sunglasses help. Leave valuables in your guesthouse and carry only a small bag or nothing at all. Your pilot will brief you on exactly what to wear or bring when you confirm your booking.

### Can I learn to fly solo here, not just do a tandem?

Yes. Several accredited paragliding schools in Bir run beginner courses that take students from no experience to solo gliding over five to eight days. These courses are popular and fill up during peak season, so book well in advance if this is your goal. Confirm instructor qualifications and that the school provides liability insurance before enrolling.

### What is the best month to visit if I can only come once?

October is the single most recommended month by experienced visitors and local operators alike. Skies are clear after the monsoon, thermals are reliable, temperatures are comfortable at both Bir and Billing, and the valley is at its most visually striking. If October is not possible, mid-March to late April is a strong alternative.
